A Little Boy's Prayer

Godspeed to the witnessed hunter,<br>
Covered in skin and wool, down to fur-laden boots,<br>
Timbered to the naked woods,<br>
Paused into sense behind his eyes,<br>
He travels to where the wild things are,<br>
Sent to deliver return to man,<br>
And to help the panting elk.<br>

Brindled hair, Dutch-braided,<br>
To be a boy is to rehearse for the foxhole,<br>
To wear the six-pointed star inside his chest,<br>
Silver as wolf piss,<br>
And to aspire to pray a hole through the ceiling.<br>
To needle his grandparents into bark,<br>
To know that the most beautiful praying sites<br>
Are the ones built by God.<br>

Sweat-soaked skin closer to heaven,<br>
With his worries left behind in the creek,<br>
His white knuckles hidden from daylight,<br>
Kneeling blindly at the hem of a ridge,<br>
The shedding of pelt inside the shed,<br>
Wings curved tight around his small existence,<br>
He has forgiveness and a heart full.<br>

He feels through the rucked foliage for the wound,<br>
The first rain wets his hands,<br>
An eyelash clings to his cheek, unblinking,<br>
His hands learn its heavy weight,<br>
Between differences of mercy,<br>
And letting something be held too long,<br>

Remembrance is a tall smokestack,<br>
Moving down his body to his stubborn hooves,<br>
And it is bearable.<br>

Raw light sets the woods alive,<br>
Bright and blue and white,<br>
A mountain bowed to necessity,<br>
The howl of a big, distant dog,<br>
Growls in his tightened chest,<br>
As the north wind crackles through the jackpines.<br>

Let the peak of antler pierce with violent passion,<br>
Rasp through birch, through his small chainmail knit,<br>
And enter his heart's summit,<br>

Between the connectedness of breath and life,<br>
This winter sun was a weak thing above the tree line,<br>
Halfhearted, small,<br>
As he seems, too.<br>

Through the glass tower,<br>
Boreal winds pass through the hunter,<br>
And river water runs down his exposed spine,<br>
Cursive arteries splayed on wooden floorboards,<br>
And his camo is fully bleached,<br>
He stands.<br>
Taller only because something beneath him is gone,<br>
This is the shepherding of the self,<br>
Bearing the sacrifice of what has been asked to take.<br>

Be brave, little boy, for February braved you.<br>

Now I sit among the stillness of a hard hush and bitter coffee,<br>
Counting hours by the shatter of lake light,<br>

Oh, I'll do the boy proud,<br>
I hunt for boyhood,<br>
And I hope I am a good man.<br>
Emunah.<br>
